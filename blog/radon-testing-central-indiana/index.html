Should I get a radon test during my home inspection in Indiana?

By Clear View Home Inspections · Published September 24, 2026 · Last reviewed September 24, 2026

Yes, in Central Indiana especially. Indiana's health department estimates nearly 1 in 3 homes statewide is above EPA's 4.0 pCi/L action level[1], and EPA maps Hamilton and Marion counties in its highest-potential zone[3][4]. Radon isn't part of a standard Indiana home inspection[5], so you have to add the test yourself.

How common is radon in Indiana?

  • The Indiana Department of Health estimates that "nearly 1 out of every 3 homes in Indiana" has radon above 4.0 pCi/L[1].
  • The American Lung Association reports that more than 40% of Indiana homes tested were at or above the action level[2].
  • EPA's Map of Radon Zones places Hamilton County (Fishers, Carmel, Noblesville) and Marion County (Indianapolis) in Zone 1, meaning a predicted average indoor level above 4 pCi/L[3][4]. EPA adds: "All homes should be tested, regardless of zone designation."[3]

Why it matters

Radon is a colorless, odorless gas and the second leading cause of lung cancer in the U.S. It's estimated to cause about 21,000 lung cancer deaths a year[1][2].

What level means action?

ResultGuidance
4.0 pCi/L or higherEPA recommends a mitigation system[2]
2.0–3.9 pCi/LEPA suggests considering mitigation[2]
Below 2.0 pCi/LRetest every five years. There's no known completely safe level[1]

Isn't radon part of the home inspection?

Not by default. Indiana's minimum standards list radon among the things a home inspector is not required to inspect[5]. You have to ask for a radon test, and it's easiest to schedule with the inspection so results arrive within your inspection window.

Who's allowed to test?

Indiana requires anyone who tests for radon to be certified by the Indiana Department of Health[6]. Testers must also agree to follow ANSI/AARST measurement protocols[7]. Ask any tester for their IDOH license number, and check it on the state's license search[6].

Buying vs. already owning

  • Buyers: test during your inspection period. HUD notes that "The Environmental Protection Agency and the Surgeon General recommend that all houses be tested for radon."[8]
  • Owners: test every five years, and every two years if you have a mitigation system[1][2].
  • Questions: Indiana Radon Hotline 1-800-272-9723[1].

Quick answers

Is radon common in Central Indiana?

Yes. Indiana's health department estimates nearly 1 in 3 Indiana homes is above 4.0 pCi/L, and EPA's radon zone map places Hamilton and Marion counties in Zone 1, the highest predicted potential.

What radon level is too high?

EPA recommends fixing a home at 4.0 pCi/L or higher and suggests considering action between 2 and 4 pCi/L.

Is radon part of a standard Indiana home inspection?

No. Indiana's standards of practice don't require home inspectors to test for radon, so it's ordered as a separate test.

Who can test for radon in Indiana?

Only testers certified by the Indiana Department of Health. You can verify a license with the state's online license search.
